This year's Capture-the-Flag threw a genuinely interesting curveball at competitors: a complete AI ban. No leaning on the tools we've all become reliant on, but it meant the organisers were free to build fun, interesting challenges that would otherwise get one-shot by an AI model.

It's so easy, when you get stuck on a problem, to just open Claude, Deepseek, Kimi, whatever, and ask what your next step should be. Without that crutch, this event reminded me of the joys of learning again: staring at code trying to figure out what it does, frantically consulting Stack Overflow, getting frustrated, and finally hitting that "aha!" moment that actually came from your own brain and not matrix multiplication. Overall a really good experience.

What made it even more rewarding was looking back at last year. There was a flag that got the better of me at Skateboarding Dog 2025, and being able to carry those lessons forward into this event felt great. I also pushed well outside my comfort zone this time round, having a real go at a PWN and a CRYPT challenge, areas I've barely touched before, and I'm excited to see where that experience takes me next year.

There were a few challenge types at the event, the ones I attempted were:
 - **GAME:** Another game!  The game challenges this year were less "Hacky" then the previous year, but still a super fun experience without being so intensive to take away from the main experience.  Ultimate I'm not going to include these in my writeupts though
 - **CRYPT:** Cryptography based
 - **PWN:** Binary Exploitation
 - **WEB:** Web Based Hacking
